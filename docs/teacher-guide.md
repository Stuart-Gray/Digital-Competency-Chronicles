# Teacher guide (draft skeleton)

This guide grows with the game. The full English and French guide for the Dungeon 2 slice is
planned for milestone 3.

## What the game is (and is not)

- A short adventure game that lets students explore the Ministry's *Content for Developing
  Digital Competency* through play.
- **Not an evaluation.** The content is not a subject and is not evaluated. The game shows no
  marks, scores or percentages. Students collect achievements and badges, and wrong answers
  lead to a hint and another try.
- Feedback happens the Ministry's way: observation, spontaneous questions and brief discussion.
  Each puzzle comes with a discussion question for you.

## Getting started

1. Open the game: https://stuart-gray.github.io/Digital-Competency-Chronicles/
2. Students press **Play**, pick a free slot, choose a character and (optionally) type their
   first name. Up to five students can save on one device.
3. They choose their cycle: **Cycle 2** (Grades 3 and 4) or **Cycle 3** (Grades 5 and 6).
   The words, scenarios and puzzles change with the cycle.
4. The game saves by itself. **Menu → Save and go to the title screen** ends a session; the
   student picks the same slot next time to continue where they left off.

Sessions are designed for 10 to 15 minutes.

## Settings for accessibility

Open **Settings** on the title screen, or **Menu → Settings** during play. Settings are saved on
the device, so they stay the same for the next student on that device (check them when devices
are shared). Full details, and how the game meets game accessibility standards, are in
[accessibility.md](accessibility.md).

**Sound and voice**
- **Music** on or off, and **Music volume** (quiet, medium, loud). On by default at medium; turn
  it off for silent work time.
- **Read aloud** reads each line with the device's own voice. When it is on, choose a **Female** or
  **Male** voice and a **Reading speed** (slow, normal, fast), and press **Try the voice** to hear it.
  The game picks the clearest voice of that kind on the device, in English or Quebec French, and
  shows its name. If a device has no voice of that kind (for example, Chromebooks have no built-in
  male French voice), the game makes another voice a little lower or higher and says so. Better
  voices can be added in the device's settings (Windows: *Time & language → Speech*; iPad:
  *Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices*).
- All game text is real web text, so Read&Write and screen readers can read it too.

**Reading and seeing**
- **Text speed**: slow, normal, fast, or *All at once*. Students always press to go on; nothing
  moves on by itself.
- **Text size**: normal, large or larger.
- **Letter style**: *Standard* (a font made for easy reading) or *Dyslexia-friendly* (OpenDyslexic,
  with more space between words and lines).
- **High contrast**: black, white and yellow, thick borders, a bigger "!" marker, a sharper game
  picture and lighter dark rooms in the temple.
- **Reduce motion**: no camera shake, fades, drifting or bouncing. *Like the device* (the default)
  follows the device's own "reduce motion" or "show animations" setting.

**Moving**
- **Walking**: *Hold to walk* (the default), or *Press to start and stop* for students who find
  holding a key or button hard. Space stops.
- **Arrow buttons on screen** (tablets): an on-screen arrow pad, if a student prefers it to tapping.

**Always there**
- Three ways to move: keyboard (arrows or WASD, Space or Enter to talk), tap or click where to go,
  and **Look around** (button at the top, or the **L** key), which lists nearby people, things and
  paths with where they are. Picking one walks there. This helps students who use a screen reader,
  a switch, or find steering hard.
- No timers, no scores, no way to fail: a wrong try gives a hint, and *Leave for now* is always
  there. Escape or **Menu** pauses at any time.
- Colour is never the only clue: hints, correct answers and choices also show a symbol.

## Privacy

No accounts, no tracking, nothing sent anywhere. Progress is saved only in the browser on the
device. The only personal information is an optional first name. **Teacher corner** (press and
hold the button on the title screen) lets you clear one save or all saves on the device.

### Helping the villagers (side quests)

The village is where students practise the two dimensions at the centre of the Framework by
helping neighbours: **dimension 1, ethical citizenship** and **dimension 2, technological
skills**. Each quest takes two to five minutes. Gus and Noor need a hand from the start; the
others move in as badge pieces come home from the temple. A small speech bubble floats over
anyone who needs help, and the village board lists who is asking. The journal keeps a list of
"People you helped" (never a list of what is left).

| Villager | Dimension, element | What students do | Ask afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gus (plaza) | D2, 3a Understand digital tools and hardware components | Match the parts of a laptop with what they do, then find out why a video has no sound | Where is the volume on the device you use at school? How can you tell the sound is off? |
| Noor (village board) | D1, 1a Recognize the social impacts of digital technology | Sort online comments on a classmate's drawing: does it help, or could it hurt? | How can you tell if a comment will help or hurt before you send it? |
| Zoé (west of the plaza, after the lantern) | D2, 4a Develop organizational skills | Choose a meaningful file name (Cycle 3: put the steps to save and find a file in order) | Try it: save a file with a meaningful name, then ask a classmate to find it. |
| Fern (garden, after the Hall of Traces) | D1, 1b Recognize the environmental impacts of digital technology | Sort habits that save or waste energy (Cycle 3: match the stages of a device's life cycle) | What is one thing our class could do to use less energy with our devices? |
| Sami (kite tree, after the Vault of Keys) | D1, 1a (generative AI) | Find the clues that a picture of a giant duck was made by an AI, then decide what could happen if it is shared | Is this real? What makes you say so? How might this image influence other people? |
| Pip (mail stall, after the Window Gallery) | D2, 3a | Find out why an email will not send: is the tablet connected to a network? | How can you tell that the device you use at school is connected to the network? |
| Bruno (bakery, after the badge) | D1, 1c Understand the factors that influence digital habits | Choose what helps when a game keeps dinging (Cycle 3: sort what pulls our attention and what helps) | What pulls you back to a game or a video? What could you do about it? |
| Rosa (pond, after the badge) | D2, 3b Understand the basic features of digital tools | Match what Rosa wants to do with the right helper: text-to-speech, voice dictation, bigger text | Which helpers on our class devices have you tried? When could they help you? |

Each wing fits a 10 to 15 minute session and ends with a badge piece; the game saves and resumes
inside the temple. The discussion questions and look-fors for area 2 are in `content/area2.json`
(`teacher`) and on each puzzle (`teacherNote`). They will appear in the teacher corner in
milestone 3, with the class progress view.
